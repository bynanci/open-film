# OpenFilm name and branding policy

OpenFilm's first-party source code is available under the Apache License, Version
2.0. This is a separate policy for avoiding confusion about the origin,
endorsement, and support of products presented under the OpenFilm identity.
It does not amend the Apache license or restrict rights to use, copy, modify,
distribute, or sell code that the license grants.

## Scope and legal status

- "OpenFilm", the project name, and the project's identifying logos are used
  to identify the upstream project and its official releases.
- Apache-2.0 section 6 does not grant general trademark rights.
- Any trademark rights and remedies depend on actual ownership, use, and
  applicable law. This document does not assert a registered trademark, an
  exclusive right to a common name, or ownership of every contributed asset.
- The underlying logo files retain their applicable copyright licenses.
  Trademark considerations about _branding_ do not cancel permission to
  reproduce copyright-licensed files.

The maintainer should complete a jurisdiction-specific name/logo clearance
search before asserting exclusive rights or seeking trademark registration.

## Known naming collision (2026-10-31)

An independent project at [openfilm/openfilm](https://github.com/openfilm/openfilm)
also publishes a video product called OpenFilm at
[openfilm.dev](https://openfilm.dev/) and has its own published
[trademark policy](https://github.com/openfilm/openfilm/blob/main/TRADEMARK.md).
This is evidence of overlapping market use, **not** a determination of
registration, infringement, ownership, or legal priority. Before commercial
branding or asserting exclusivity, check existing rights in each intended
market and consider adopting a more distinctive identifier.

## Uses that generally do not require special approval

- Truthfully identifying OpenFilm as the origin of source code.
- Linking to the upstream repository, reproducing required attribution, and
  describing compatibility (for example, "based on OpenFilm").
- Discussing, comparing, teaching, or reviewing the project and using names
  solely as reasonably necessary to identify it.
- Redistributing an unchanged upstream release with accurate provenance,
  without implying a separate distributor is the project maintainer.

These examples are illustrative. Applicable nominative, descriptive, or other
lawful uses remain available regardless of this policy.

## Uses that require a separate branding review

Where the project has enforceable rights in the relevant identifier, obtain
written permission before using its name or identifying logo as the _primary
brand_ of a materially modified fork, paid service, or independent commercial
product. Do not suggest that a third-party product is "official", "certified",
sponsored, or supported by OpenFilm without authorization.

Choose distinct product branding for materially modified forks while retaining
all copyright attribution required by the software licenses. A clear statement
such as "Built using OpenFilm; not affiliated with the upstream maintainers"
helps prevent confusion. These branding guidelines are not additional
copyright-license conditions.

## Permission requests and disputes

Open an issue in the upstream repository to request a private contact method
for branding questions; do not post confidential business information or
credentials in a public issue. Approval must be explicit and in writing;
silence is not permission. Decisions are limited to rights the relevant
rights-holder actually controls.

This policy cannot retroactively revoke Apache-2.0 copyright permissions or
override applicable trademark exceptions. For a specific jurisdiction or
dispute, seek advice from a qualified intellectual-property professional.
