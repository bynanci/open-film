# Commercial use and product boundaries

OpenFilm is an open-source project. Its current authorized first-party code is
licensed under Apache-2.0. Commercial use is already permitted, including
internal business use, paid distribution, services, and proprietary derivatives
that comply with the license. A company does not need to buy an OpenFilm license
merely to exercise those rights.

## What remains open

- The Apache-2.0 code, its license, and required attribution remain available
  to all downstream users, including competitors.
- Commercial distributors must comply with LICENSE, applicable NOTICE
  attribution, modified-file notices, and each relevant third-party license.
- A paid offering cannot revoke previous grants or make existing open-source
  revisions exclusive.
- The license does not provide a support contract, warranty, trademark license,
  access to private services, or an endorsement by the maintainers.

See the authoritative [LICENSE](LICENSE), [NOTICE](NOTICE), and
[licensing guide](docs/licensing.md).

## Potential commercial offerings

These are possible future offerings, **not launched products or promises**:

- Paid installation, training, technical support, maintenance, and SLA services.
- Hosted workflows and managed rendering or collaboration infrastructure.
- Separately developed premium integrations, enterprise administration, or
  proprietary add-ons, provided third-party licenses are respected.
- Official, branded distributions and commercial partnerships subject to
  separate contractual and branding terms.

The current core does not become closed-source when a paid service launches.
Whether a specific extension is a separate work or a derivative must be
evaluated using its actual architecture and applicable licenses.

## Contribution ownership and future dual licensing

Contributors retain their copyrights unless they enter a separate written
agreement. OpenFilm's contribution process uses the Developer Certificate
of Origin (DCO) to document provenance; **DCO sign-off is not an assignment
of copyright or a grant of exclusive relicensing rights**.

Apache-2.0 already permits commercial use and certain proprietary downstream
distributions. If the project later wants _separate proprietary licensing on
terms beyond rights already granted_, it must verify rights in each relevant
contribution and obtain any necessary permissions or contributor agreements.
A repository license label or maintainer status is not a substitute for
chain-of-title evidence. Historical GPL-3.0-only grants and third-party
obligations also remain relevant to the material they cover.

## Official product identity

The [branding policy](TRADEMARKS.md) explains how to avoid confusing an
unofficial fork or third-party service with an official OpenFilm release.
This is separate from code copyright permissions. No trademark registration
or exclusive ownership of the project name is asserted here.

For governance, see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md). For security reports,
see [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md). Product pricing, paid-feature scope, and
possible contributor agreements require separate decisions and review.
